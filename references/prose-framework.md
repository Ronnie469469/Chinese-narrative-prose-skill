# Prose Framework

Use this as the base architecture under the New Concept Writing style layer.

## Input Reading

Extract these fields from the user's request:

- Theme: the visible subject.
- Material: real memories, scenes, people, objects, facts, time span, and setting.
- Desire: what the piece should make the reader feel or understand.
- Reader: private self, public readers, family member, friend, admissions/contest audience, brand audience, or unspecified.
- Boundary: facts not to invent, people not to expose, tone to avoid, length limit.

If the user gives only a theme, ask for three concrete details:

1. One scene or moment.
2. One person, object, or place that matters.
3. The emotional direction of the ending.

## Theme Framework

Build every piece around two sentences:

- **Surface sentence:** This essay appears to be about X.
- **Deep sentence:** It is actually about Y becoming visible through X.

Examples of useful deep movements:

- Missing someone becomes understanding the old form of oneself.
- Leaving a place becomes discovering what cannot be packed.
- Family conflict becomes learning the grammar of another generation's love.
- A city scene becomes a question about how people remain connected.

Do not state the deep sentence too early in the essay. Let the body discover it.

## Narrative Framework

Use a five-part arc:

1. **Opening aperture:** one concrete present-tense detail.
2. **First return:** memory, background, or relationship context.
3. **Scene chain:** three to six scenes linked by object, phrase, place, or repeated action.
4. **Recognition turn:** the narrator's understanding changes, usually through a small event rather than a speech.
5. **Afterimage:** ending detail that holds the new meaning.

Avoid explaining the entire background before the first scene. Let the reader enter through a visible moment.

## Scene Framework

Each main scene should contain:

- Where we are.
- Who is present or absent.
- What someone physically does.
- What the narrator notices but does not yet understand.
- One detail that can return later.

A scene fails if it only says how the narrator feels.

## Emotion Framework

Use restraint by delaying direct naming:

- First third: observe and establish ordinary life.
- Middle third: increase pressure through repetition, contrast, or absence.
- Final third: allow recognition, but keep the language cooler than the emotion.

If the material is intense, reduce adjectives and increase action. If the material is quiet, strengthen the object thread and sentence rhythm.

## Language Framework

Use three registers in balance:

- Plain narration: clear, daily, almost spoken.
- Exact observation: small nouns and verbs that make the world visible.
- Lyrical compression: brief sentences that compress feeling after enough evidence.

Revision priorities:

1. Replace abstract emotion with scene.
2. Replace generic nouns with exact objects.
3. Cut repeated explanations.
4. Break long paragraphs at emotional turns.
5. Remove decorative metaphors that do no work.

## Output Contract

For generation:

- `文章命题`: one to three sentences naming the surface and deep movement.
- `叙事提纲`: five to eight beats, each with scene purpose and emotional movement.
- `完整正文`: title plus body.
- `风格自检`: concise rubric notes.

For rewrite:

- Briefly identify the current draft's strongest material.
- Provide the rewritten version.
- Add concrete notes on what changed and why.

For critique:

- Score and diagnose according to `revision-rubric.md`.
- Provide a prioritized revision plan.
- Include sample rewritten fragments only when useful.
