# Mode Selector

Select one primary mode before drafting. Mention the selected mode only if it helps the user understand the output.

## 1. Growth And Retrospection

Use when the prompt involves graduation, age, childhood, an old habit, a changed self, or belated understanding.

- Narrator: usually first person.
- Movement: past self misreads something; present self sees the cost or tenderness.
- Texture: school corridors, notebooks, exams, old clothes, season changes, repeated routes.
- Ending: return to the younger self without judging them.

## 2. Hometown And Departure

Use when the prompt involves leaving, returning, migration, railway stations, old neighborhoods, family houses, or local speech.

- Narrator: first person or close third.
- Movement: departure seems spatial, then becomes a change in identity.
- Texture: station broadcasts, luggage, food, doors, roads, dialect, weather, old rooms.
- Ending: the place remains ordinary; the narrator's relation to it changes.

## 3. Family And Kinship

Use when the prompt involves parents, grandparents, siblings, meals, illness, care, conflict, silence, or inheritance.

- Narrator: first person with restrained distance.
- Movement: misunderstanding or irritation becomes recognition of another person's limits.
- Texture: kitchens, hands, medicine, bills, phone calls, repeated sayings, household objects.
- Ending: an ordinary gesture carries love without naming it too loudly.

## 4. Youth, Relationship, And Missed Timing

Use when the prompt involves friendship, first love, classmates, unsent messages, rivalry, apology, or parting.

- Narrator: first person, sometimes second person if writing to an absent "you".
- Movement: intimacy is shown through small rituals; loss appears through changed rhythm.
- Texture: messages, seats, shared routes, borrowed objects, nicknames, music, unfinished conversations.
- Ending: leave one thing unsaid or let a small sign remain.

## 5. City, Solitude, And Daily Drift

Use when the prompt involves living alone, work, commuting, rented rooms, night streets, crowds, screens, or anonymous encounters.

- Narrator: first person with observational distance.
- Movement: the city looks indifferent, then reveals fragile connections.
- Texture: elevators, convenience stores, subway stations, delivery bags, windows, neon, phone batteries.
- Ending: no grand rescue; a small act or perception changes the loneliness.

## 6. Social Observation Through Daily Life

Use when the prompt involves technology, public events, generational change, class difference, education, media, or a social issue.

- Narrator: first person witness, not commentator.
- Movement: begin with a small personal scene, widen carefully, return to one person or object.
- Texture: forms, apps, queues, schools, hospitals, workplaces, online language, public notices.
- Ending: hold the question open; avoid editorial conclusions.

## Secondary Mode Rules

- Hometown often combines with Family.
- Youth often combines with Growth.
- City often combines with Social Observation.
- Use no more than two modes. More modes usually blur the emotional center.

## Mode Selection Priority

1. If a relationship is central, choose the relationship mode first.
2. If place is central, choose Hometown or City.
3. If the material is mainly reflective with weak plot, choose Growth and build scenes around objects.
4. If the prompt names a public issue, choose Social Observation but keep the narrator embodied.
